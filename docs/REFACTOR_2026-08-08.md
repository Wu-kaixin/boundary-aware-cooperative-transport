## 1. What was actually wrong

The roadmap's central claim is that the failure was not in any single module but
in the numerical relations *between* modules, and that turned out to be right —
though the specific relations were not all the ones anticipated. Six defects were
found, each of which independently made the stated task impossible:

| # | Defect | How it presented | Where it is now fixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cage radius outside the contact band | Robots converge to `d_c` and never touch the cargo; every safety metric reports a perfect run | `contracts.ContactSafetyContract` (C1) |
| 2 | Object barrier built from every mapped point | Tangent plane of a point around a corner cuts a robot with 0.36 m of true clearance; QP infeasible on 65 of 600 solves | `safety_filter`, tangential window + inner limit |
| 3 | Viscous floor drag | Cargo's terminal speed exceeds the robots' speed limit, so it is kicked away by the first robot to touch it; minimum clearance came out at exactly `r_robot` in **all 12** configurations of a parameter sweep | `contact_dynamics`, Coulomb friction with stiction |
| 4 | Limited-range CVT local equilibrium | Robots arriving from one side converge onto the arc they can see; strict coverage pinned at 0.63 with nothing on the trailing face | `controller._redeploy_step` |
| 5 | Uniform cage offset | Leading robots are in contact too, and their normal force cancels the pushing arc: goal-direction components of +1.33, +1.89 and −3.31 N, net −0.09 N against a 12.7 N breakaway force | `DensityParams.offsets_for`, graded lift |
| 6 | Transport bias along `û_goal` | Inward only at the centre of the trailing face, tangential elsewhere; raising the gain from 0.18 to 1.50 shrank the pushing arc from 5 robots to 1 and turned net goal force from +3.55 to −3.75 N | `controller._transport_bias`, press along `−n̂` |

Defects 2–6 were **not** in any of the three source documents. They are reported
here because each was found by an assertion or a measurement that the refactor
introduced, which is the case for the contract layer being worth its cost.

## 2. The contract layer

A contract is a numerical relation between quantities owned by different layers,
asserted where it can still fail cheaply. All three are in
[`src/dbact/contracts.py`](../src/dbact/contracts.py) and all are checked before a
controller is constructed.

### C1 — contact/safety

```
r_safe = r_robot − δ_max   <   d_c   <   r_robot
γ_obj · (d_c − r_safe)  >  ρ
d_min  ≥  2 r_robot
lead_offset  >  r_robot            (transport only)
```

The first line is the roadmap's. The last two were added here:

- `d_min ≥ 2 r_robot` — the robots are modelled as discs by the contact model but
  as points by the inter-robot barrier. With `d_min = 0.30` and `r_robot = 0.16`
  they overlapped while the barrier reported safety.
- `lead_offset > r_robot` — see defect 5. Without it, net progress is
  structurally impossible regardless of gains, so it belongs in the contract
  rather than in a tuning note.

Paper configuration: `r_robot = 0.16`, `δ_max = 0.05`, `d_c = 0.135`,
`γ_obj = 8.0`, `ρ = 0.05`, `d_min = 0.34`, `lead_offset = 0.22`. Barrier margin
`γ_obj(d_c − r_safe) − ρ = +0.15`.

### C2 — solver provenance, fail-closed

There is no `auto` backend. Three explicit ones:

| backend | meaning |
|---|---|
| `qp` | exact analytic planar QP ([`qp2d.py`](../src/dbact/qp2d.py)), no external dependency |
| `cvxpy` | same problem through cvxpy; the independent cross-check |
| `projection` | iterated half-plane projection; inexact, must be asked for by name |

`qp` is exact rather than iterative: in two dimensions the projection onto the
feasible set is attained at the unconstrained optimum, a perpendicular projection
onto one constraint, or an intersection of two, so enumerating those candidates
gives the optimum with no tolerance at which the answer silently degrades.
`tests/test_qp2d.py` checks it against cvxpy on 600 random problems, including
agreement on *infeasibility* verdicts.

The filter carries **no slack variable**. Feasibility certificate: with `u_i = 0`
the inter-robot rows hold whenever `h_ij ≥ 0` and the object rows hold whenever
`γ_obj h_k ≥ n̂ᵀv̂_obj + ρ`. Reported `max_slack` is therefore `0.0` by
construction, not by tuning — a soft quadratic penalty could never produce that.

**Two-tier solve.** When the ISSf margin `ρ` is itself what makes the problem
infeasible, the solve is retried with `ρ` removed. The safety property is
`h ≥ 0`; `ρ` is the robustness margin absorbing the neglected `d/dt(n̂)ᵀ(p−b)`
term. Dropping it keeps `ḣ ≥ −γh`, so the barrier survives while the ISSf
constant does not — and the relaxation count is reported, because the ISSf claim
only covers steps where the margin was enforced.

**Certificate accounting.** The zero-input certificate is evaluated against the
margin-free right-hand side, and the two situations are counted separately:

| statistic | meaning |
|---|---|
| `inside_margin_band` | `u = 0` satisfies the barrier but not the margin. **This is the intended operating point at the cage ring**, not a violation — the band exists to demand active retreat. |
| `zero_input_feasible_failures` | `u = 0` does not satisfy the barrier. Real information. |

Getting this wrong is easy and was gotten wrong here first: evaluating the
certificate *with* `ρ` included reported 5128 failures out of 14 400, which reads
as a broken construction. Separating the two gives 4530 in the margin band and 598
genuine dips — and those 598 are the discrete-time overshoot of the same
inequality that item 4 above allows for, on a run where no robot ever entered the
cargo. The rate is reported rather than asserted to be zero; what is asserted
exactly is `max_agents_inside == 0`.

### C3 — success criterion

`J = (x_T − x_0)·û_goal` replaces `‖Δx‖`. Five conditions, all required:

1. `J ≥ J_min`
2. `J/‖Δx‖ ≥ 0.7`, evaluated only when `‖Δx‖ ≥ 0.1 m` (direction is close to
   undefined below that)
3. `min_t signed_clearance ≥ 0`
4. `max_t penetration ≤ δ_max + dt(v_robot + v_cargo)` — the barrier holds in
   continuous time and a fixed-step integrator can overshoot by one step of
   relative motion; that allowance is *stated* rather than folded into `δ_max`
5. zero solver fallbacks, and `min_t d_ij ≥ d_min`

Failures are recorded as reason strings, not a boolean.
`tests/test_contracts.py` includes the V2 counter-example directly: a 0.6230 m
displacement at 170.11° passes a magnitude test and fails this one.

### Initial-state check

Added here, not in the roadmap. A barrier certificate is about *maintaining*
safety from a safe initial set, so a run starting outside it demonstrates nothing
about the filter. `assert_initial_state_valid` rejects overlapping robots and
robots starting inside a cargo. This caught a `layout: random` start that placed
two robots 0.026 m apart — the reported minimum separation was being set by the
initial condition, not by the filter.

## 3. S1–S6 results

All numbers below are the output of
`python scripts/verify_refactor.py --stage all --steps 900 --json runs/verify/verify.json`
on the committed configs, seed 0. Stage verdicts from that run: **S1–S6 PASS, S7
FAIL** (see [§4](#4-s7--directional-transport-the-honest-result)).

### S1 safety — `safety_filter.py`

Inter-robot rows in shared-responsibility form (Wang, Ames & Egerstedt, T-RO
2017 — **a known result, used to support the feasibility proposition, not
claimed**). Object rows one per nearby observed boundary point, as an
intersection of half-spaces rather than a smooth CBF, following the nonsmooth
barrier construction of Glotfelter, Cortés & Egerstedt (L-CSS 2017).

Two admission rules were necessary and are not in any source document:

- **Tangential window** `W = 0.28`. A local plane describes the boundary only
  near where it was fitted. Without the window the construction silently becomes
  a convex-hull constraint. It also bounds the effect of normal error: a normal
  wrong by `ε_n` misplaces the plane by at most `W sin ε_n`, which is how the
  perception error budget becomes a safety margin.
- **Inner limit** `= r_robot`. A robot's map holds relayed points from the *far*
  face of a thin part, and a robot safely outside one face is on the inner side of
  the opposite face's plane. On the L shape (leg thickness 0.405 m) this demanded
  a full-speed retreat from a robot with 0.16 m of clearance.

Same scenario, same seed, 900 steps, with the object rows on and off:

| | legacy (no object CBF) | S1 |
|---|---|---|
| robots inside the cargo | **9 of 16** | **0 of 16** |
| min signed clearance | **−0.3669 m** | **+0.1067 m** |
| max penetration | **0.5269 m** | **0.0533 m** (budget 0.0734) |
| max cargo speed | 1.297 m/s | 0.167 m/s |
| strict coverage | 0.8875 | **1.0000** |
| **legacy coverage** | **1.0000** | 1.0000 |
| `max_slack` | 0.0 (rows absent) | **0.0** |
| certificate failure rate | — | 4.15% (598 / 14 400), all within the discretisation bound |

The penetration budget is `δ_max + dt(v_robot + v_cargo)` with the cargo speed
*measured* rather than taken from the engine's clamp: 0.05 + 0.05(0.30 + 0.167) =
**0.0734 m**, against a measured 0.0533 m. Using the clamp instead would have given
a 0.165 m budget — loose enough to be meaningless — so the bound is computed from
the run it is applied to.

The two coverage rows are the whole argument for the metric change. With the
barrier disabled, 9 of 16 robots end up *inside* the cargo — and the legacy metric
scores that run **1.000**, higher than the strict metric scores it, because a robot
that has passed through the boundary is close to many boundary samples at once.
Any pre-S1 coverage comparison is therefore uninterpretable, which is why they are
all withdrawn rather than adjusted.

### S2 perception — `perception.py`

Ray casting, nearest hit per ray across all objects, so self-occlusion and
inter-object occlusion both fall out. Normals from local PCA on the returns.

**Confidence is a fit residual, not an eigenvalue ratio.**
`σ_r = sqrt(λ_min)`, `c = clip(1 − σ_r/tol, 0, 1)`. The ratio form fails because
ray-scan spacing is anisotropic enough that a corner still looks linear by ratio;
the residual form carries metres, so tightening the tolerance provably rejects
more (`tests/test_perception.py`).

Measured over 44 viewpoints on the L shape, at a 3σ line-of-sight tolerance:

| | occluded / returns | rate |
|---|---|---|
| legacy sampler | 339 / 1056 | **32.1%** |
| ray cast | 1 / 1244 | **0.08%** |

The residual 0.08% is the genuine tail of the range-noise distribution beyond 3σ,
not see-through sensing, so the criterion is a stated rate rather than exact zero.

Normal error, before and after the confidence gate: p90 **8.30° → 7.33°**, and
gross errors above 30° **40 → 29**.

A real bug was found here by the test: with zero noise the return lies exactly on
the boundary, so a zero-tolerance audit reports a *perfect* sensor as 100%
occluded. `occlusion_rate` now always adds a geometric epsilon.

The legacy sampler's normal error is **identically zero** — it returns the
simulator's normals — which is why any robustness margin derived against it is
vacuous.

### S3 map — `boundary_map.py`

Voxel key `(object_id, round(b/v))`, one fused record per cell. Confidence-weighted
mean position and normal, **max** confidence, arc length **capped at the cell
diagonal** per source. Age enters at read time as `exp(−λ_age Δt)`.

Verified: 20 repeated updates and an 8× relay both leave cell count, total arc
length and integrated density mass **bit-identical** to a single update. Capacity
applies to cells, so it bounds spatial extent rather than update rate.

### S4 density — `boundary_density.py`

Four physical factors (arc length, confidence, age, uncovered gap) and two models
behind `density_mode`. The distance-field level set is the boundary of a Minkowski
sum, so it cannot self-intersect; measured mass inside the object is strictly
lower than the offset model's on the L shape. Cost, reported as such: total mass
is no longer proportional to estimated perimeter.

Correction to the roadmap: on a polygon with a *sharp* reflex corner the folded
offset targets stay outside the outline, so "5% of targets inside the object" does
not reproduce. The measurable symptom is the concave-corner mass pile-up
(peak/median), and that is what is reported.

### S5 coverage — `local_cvt.py`

Strict disk `Ω_i = D ∩ B(p_i, R_l)`; truncated `f(r) = min(r², R_l²)`;
`H = ∫_D min_i f(‖q−p_i‖) φ dq` with mass outside every disk charged at `R_l²`.

- Max sample distance from the robot: **0.795 m for `R_l = 0.80`** (was 7.16 m for
  `R_l = 1.6`, because the old integration domain was unioned with the bounding
  box of every density target).
- Descent over 79 steps: at gain 0.25, `H` falls **1.0128 → 0.3346** with **0
  rising steps**; at gain 0.60, 0 rising steps; at gain 1.00, **7 rising steps**.
  The step-size bound is real and is measured in both directions.
- Neighbour completeness `R_l ≤ R_comm/2` warns when violated.
- A single robot converges to `d_c` to **four decimal places** for every σ and
  `R_l` tested, which is what makes C1 a statement about a distance the robot
  actually reaches.

### S6 physics — `contact_dynamics.py`, `transport_dynamics.py`

Cargo is a rigid body with rotation; robots are kinematic discs; contact is a
penalty model with Coulomb friction at the contact and **Coulomb friction at the
floor**. Three falsification tests pass:

| test | result |
|---|---|
| push from the left, configured direction `(0,−1)` | displacement `(2.022, 3.1e−17)`, i.e. **exactly 90.0°** from the configured direction |
| same scenario, `engine: scripted` | **0.000000°** from the configured direction |
| single off-centre push | rotation **−30.18°** (the legacy cargo could not rotate at all) |
| all robots far away | displacement **exactly 0.00e+00** |

The first two rows are the same scenario under the two engines. Under contact
dynamics the configured direction is orthogonal to what happens; under the scripted
engine it *is* what happens. That is the cleanest available statement of why the
pre-refactor transport numbers were a restatement of their configuration.

`Cargo` no longer has a `transport_direction` field at all, so "the cargo moved
the way the config said" is not an outcome the physics is able to produce.

**Gate 1 mechanical spike, both engines:**

| engine | displacement along push direction |
|---|---|
| `penalty` | **13.49 m** |
| `pymunk` | **13.40 m** |

0.7% apart across two independent contact solvers. The mechanism has ample
capacity; whatever limits the closed loop is not the mechanism.

## 4. S7 — directional transport: the honest result

**The reverse-displacement failure is fixed. The task threshold is not met.**

`configs/sim/v2/l_shape_v2.yaml` as committed, 16 robots, L shape at scale 1.5,
900 steps (45 s), seed 0:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| strict boundary coverage | **1.0000** |
| robots inside the cargo | **0** |
| min signed clearance | **+0.1067 m** |
| max penetration | 0.0533 m (budget 0.0734) |
| min inter-robot distance | **0.340000 m** (`d_min = 0.34`) |
| solver | 14 399 `optimal`, **1** `fallback_projection` |
| directional progress `J` | **+0.0567 m** |
| displacement `‖Δx‖` | 0.0629 m |
| progress efficiency `J/‖Δx‖` | **0.902** |
| angle to `û_goal` | **25.6°** (V2: 170.11°) |
| mean / max contacts | 2.71 / 8 of 16 |
| peak net force | 50.9 N (breakaway 31.1 N) |
| modes at the end | 8 `cage`, 8 `push` |
| **C3 verdict** | **FAIL** — `J = 0.0567 < J_min = 0.15`; plus the 1 fallback |

`J` reaches ≈0.057 and stops. This is not slow progress, it is a **static
equilibrium** — once the cage re-forms around the new pose, the pushing arc's net
force drops back below the breakaway threshold.

Measured at the committed `mu_g = 0.77` (breakaway 31.1 N): `J` is 0.0567 m at 900
steps and 0.0575 m at 1400 steps, i.e. 0.0008 m over the next 25 s. A separate
3000-step run at `mu_g = 0.45` (breakaway 18.2 N, cooperating robots required
2.49 → 1.45) reached a larger plateau, 0.062 m, and was **flat from t = 60 s to
t = 150 s**. So cutting the force the team has to overcome by a third moves the
plateau slightly and does not remove it.

**It is therefore not a force-budget problem**, and raising the transport gain makes
it worse rather than better (defect 6). Both are the signature of hypothesis 6: what
is missing is a controller that regulates the net *wrench*, not a better setting of
one that regulates positions.

### Table 7 hypothesis audit

The roadmap's ordered checklist, with what was measured:

| # | Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | C1 violated: cage ring outside the contact band | **CONFIRMED, fixed** | mean contacts 0.00 → 2.71 (max 8); peak net force 0.0 → 50.9 N |
| 2 | Net force from one-sided residual asymmetry | **CONFIRMED, fixed** | goal-force components +1.33/+1.89/−3.31 N → net −0.09 N with a uniform cage; graded lift makes the leading arc non-resisting |
| 3 | Bias direction sign error | **REJECTED** | sign correct; but the bias *frame* was wrong (defect 6) |
| 4 | Map smearing puts density behind the cargo | **NOT REACHED** | displacement too small for age decay to matter |
| 5 | CBF pushes robots off the push direction | **REJECTED** | penetration reaches the full `δ_max` budget; the barrier is not the limit |
| 6 | The control law genuinely needs a wrench layer | **CONFIRMED, partially addressed** | push/lateral/leading allocation implemented; still a quasi-static equilibrium |

So: C1 was necessary but, exactly as the roadmap predicted, not sufficient — and
hypothesis 6 is the residual. What remains is not a tuning problem. A cage of
*kinematic* robots at a fixed offset has a static equilibrium at every pose; a
closed-loop wrench controller that regulates the net wrench rather than each
robot's position is a different control problem, and the roadmap's 5–7 day budget
for S7 is the right order of magnitude for it.

### Consequences under §9.1

Per the roadmap's own No-Go rule, the topic contracts to **boundary enclosure and
contact formation**, and that contracted topic is fully supported by what is
measured here:

- all three claimed contributions are exercised and none depends on transport;
- `configs/sim/v2/l_shape_enclosure_v2.yaml` is the corresponding scenario;
- the 13.4 m spike belongs in discussion as evidence of mechanical capacity, not
  as a closed-loop result;
- venue: JIRS/RAS rather than L-CSS, as §9.1 says.

`J > 0` at 0.90 efficiency is still worth reporting: it shows the boundary-aware
density produces a usable pushing configuration, which is the substantive claim. It
does not show closed-loop transport, and it is not written up as though it does.

## 5. Withdrawn

Per the roadmap's §9.2 retraction list:

- **"end-to-end transport success"** — withdrawn. C3 fails on `J`.
- **"3600 calls all hard-QP"** — the statement is now *checkable* rather than
  assumed. On the committed paper config over 900 steps: 14 399 of 14 400 solves
  `optimal`, **1** `fallback_projection`. That single fallback makes the run
  **invalid** under C2, and `validate_run.py` rejects it on exactly that ground. It
  is reported rather than rounded away — an infeasibility rate of 1 in 14 400 is
  still a rate, and the honest form of the claim names it.
- **All pre-S1 coverage numbers**, including the 0.988 on
  `one_nonconvex_polygon_caging_tight` — void. They counted robots standing
  inside the cargo as covering its boundary. Every legacy config was migrated by
  `scripts/migrate_configs.py`, which carries that statement in its header.
- **"main venue decided"** — withdrawn, per §9.1.
- **Any "stable"/"robust" claim** — no multi-seed experiment has been run yet;
  Gate 4 is not started.

## 6. Leftover theory (roadmap §10)

- **10.1 no common Lyapunov function.** Each robot has its own `φ_i`, so `Σ_i H_i`
  is not a coverage functional and the system is a game. Unaddressed in code; the
  roadmap's route (b) (treat `φ_i − φ̄` as a bounded disturbance, ISS/practical
  convergence) remains the recommendation, and it composes with the `ε_n` bound.
- **10.2 ISSf rather than exact CBF.** `v̂_obj` is a differenced observed
  centroid, biased when the visible arc changes. `object_velocity_filter`
  low-passes it; the bound on `‖v̂_obj − v_obj‖` is still owed.
- **10.3 deadlock.** `u_i = 0` is always feasible, so the QP always has a
  solution and deadlock is the price. Two mechanisms now escape it (`approach`,
  `redeploy`) and their activation counts are logged per step; a deliberate
  deadlock-rate experiment is still owed.
- **10.4 recruitment had no mechanism.** **Now it does.** Standard CVT keeps all
  N robots engaged forever and only redistributes them. The cell-mass test in
  `_redeploy_step` moves a robot off boundary that neighbours already hold, which
  is the exit mechanism the claim needed. This is the one §10 item the refactor
  closes rather than documents.

## 9. What is next

Gate 4 (multi-seed main experiment plus ablations) is not started, and until it
runs no stability or robustness claim is supported. `scripts/run_batch.py` exists
and reports the rejection rate alongside the surviving statistics, so the sweep is
a single command; it has deliberately not been run and summarised yet, because
doing so before the S7 decision would produce a table nobody should cite.

Before it runs, S7 needs the decision §9.1 describes:

1. **Build the wrench layer.** Hypothesis 6 is the residual and the evidence points
   at it precisely: with the arcs allocated and the pushers at full penetration, the
   configuration is a static equilibrium at every pose. What is missing is a
   controller that regulates the net wrench on the object rather than each robot's
   position. The roadmap's 5–7 day budget is the right order of magnitude, and it is
   genuinely new control work rather than tuning.
2. **Contract to enclosure.** Supported by every measurement here today. All three
   claimed contributions survive intact, `l_shape_enclosure_v2.yaml` is the
   scenario, the 13.4 m spike goes in discussion as mechanical capacity, and the
   venue becomes JIRS/RAS.

The remaining single `fallback_projection` in 14 400 solves should also be closed
out either way: it is one constraint conflict during cage formation, and while it
