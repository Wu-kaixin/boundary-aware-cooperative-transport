# L06 — Collaborative Planar Pushing of Polytopic Objects with Multiple Robots in Complex Scenes

Zili Tang, Yuming Feng, Meng Guo; arXiv:2405.07908v2, 1 June 2024. Full extracted PDF pp. 1–16 read; original equations/figures pending visual verification. Primary arXiv metadata confirms RSS 2024, Article 63, DOI 10.15607/RSS.2024.XX.063: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07908 .

**Capability:** contact-mode selection/reconfiguration and pose tracking for polytopic objects in clutter. Known shape, center of mass, mass, inertia, pressure distribution and friction, centralized perception (p. 2 and p. 15 limits); can adapt to later-observed/dynamic obstacles, so do not claim it only handles fixed known scenes.

**Mechanism:** quasi-static ellipsoidal limit-surface analysis; multidirectional force feasibility, sparse LP contact generation, keyframe hybrid search and NMPC plus mode replanning (pp. 3–10). Mode set must positively span generalized velocities for Theorems 1–2. Approximation/completeness concerns an existing collision-free guiding path and sufficient modes; it is not unconditional arbitrary-object success.

**Evidence:** randomized 50 trials per method in each of three scenes, comparisons to adapted occlusion, angular, single-direction and uniform-segmentation baselines (p. 14, Table I); multiple fleet sizes, failures, temporary localization blackout, known non-polytopic objects and 3D extensions (pp. 12–13). Hardware: three LIMO robots, OptiTrack global poses, centralized laptop, L/rectangle cases with average errors 0.052/0.023 m (pp. 14–15).

**DBACT consequence:** shape handling, contact reconfiguration, force-aware deployment, disturbance response and physical pushing are already strong prior capabilities. A possible contribution requires doing a specified subset with substantially less object information, while retaining measurable task capability. Current DBACT completion/engine results do not establish that yet.
