# L15 — Distributed Centroid Estimation and Motion Controllers for Collective Transport by Multi-Robot Systems

Golnaz Habibi, Zachary Kingston, William Xie, Mathew Jellins, James McLurkin; ICRA 2015 (venue confirmed by corpus citations; publisher metadata pending). Full extracted PDF pp. 1–7 read. Figures/equations require visual verification. Hardware relative range was supplied by AprilTag and broadcast, while onboard IR supplied bearings/orientations (p. 5); this is an important practical information distinction.

**Task/information:** unknown geometry and size; manipulators already surround and attach to object by pin joints. A connected relative-pose communication graph, IDs, multi-hop trees and a guide robot are assumed (p. 2). No shared global coordinate frame needed. Tree aggregation estimates *robot-position mean*, used as an object centroid approximation; that mean is generally neither polygon area centroid nor center of mass. Do not import that approximation as an exact identity.

**Mechanism:** one tree rooted at every manipulator; O(n) messages per robot per round, errors reset in O(2 diameter) rounds. Pivot rotation, centroid rotation, translation and combined trochoid primitives (pp. 3–4). Initial free deployment is outside the controller tests.

**Evidence:** centroid estimate 90 trials, 2–9 robots and five objects; mean 4.64% (p. 3, Fig. 4). Motion tests 96 trials with 5–7 robots (p. 6); compound controller uses holes rather than ordinary grippers due to friction constraints. No end-to-end obstacle transport guarantee; collision avoidance/integration listed as open (p. 7).

**DBACT use:** estimated geometry for local cooperative transport has prior hardware evidence. Distinguish boundary-observation registration from robot centroid approximation, and distinguish free placement from already attached manipulation.
