# L21 — Occlusion-Based Cooperative Transport for Concave Objects with a Swarm of Miniature Mobile Robots

Source: Sanjuksha Nirgude, Animesh Nema, Aishwary Jagetia; arXiv:2409.02436v1, 4 September 2024, 7 PDF pages. [Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1D473DTOjXiJBIX99QGPdIsZkBV5G1c-f/view). Status: full extracted text read, pp. 1–7; figures not yet visually verified.

- **Task:** proposed concavity filling followed by goal-occlusion pushing. No prior shape, no explicit inter-robot messages; importantly transport experiments use own and dynamically updated object position (pp. 2, 5), rather than range sensing alone.
- **Mechanism (pp. 2–3):** wall following; detect a greater-than-pi concavity from proximity sensors, freeze robots into a pseudo-object until effectively convex; other robots push when the goal light is occluded. Four proximity returns substitute for the theoretical five in the implementation.
- **Evidence (pp. 3–6):** ARGoS, concavity filling on U/L/arc shapes and separate convex-object transport; the two stages were **not integrated** (p. 7, future work). Grid/random distributions and wall proximity are discussed qualitatively; no published success-count table in this PDF.
- **Limits (pp. 6–7):** confuses robots, wall and object; finite proximity range; enough filling robots needed; poor behavior near walls; no formal collision or caging guarantee. An integrated concave transport capability is proposed, not demonstrated by these experiments.
- **Effect on DBACT:** concave objects, local reactive deployment and communication-free pushing are not sufficient novelty. A useful distinction could be shape-preserving deployment without sacrificial cavity filling, but requires a matched task, information and measured success; it is not yet demonstrated by DBACT's current transport results.
- **Use:** closest concavity/occlusion comparison, with proposed versus separately demonstrated stages explicitly distinguished. Follow citation to Chen et al. 2015 and Rubenstein et al. 2013.
