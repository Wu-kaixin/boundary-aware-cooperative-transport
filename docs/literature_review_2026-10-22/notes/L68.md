# L68 — SEAMLiS: Visibility-Aware Safety for Perception-Limited Multi-Robot Exploration

Taekyung Kim, Rahul H Kumar, Aswin D. Menon, Tzu-Hsiang Lin, Dimitra Panagou; arXiv:2607.09959v1, 10 July 2026. Full extracted text pp. 1–17 read. Primary metadata: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09959 . PDF p. 15 visually verified. Remaining key proof figures/formulas pending visual review.

**Task/information (pp. 4–5):** decentralized exploration of initially unknown static obstacles; finite range/FoV, bounded acceleration, decoupled yaw. Local maps distinguish known-free, occupied, unknown. Bounded conservative state/mapping errors and correct classification within footprint are assumed; neighbors exchange poses/frontiers, not full maps.

**Mechanism (pp. 7–10):** first planned-path crossing of known-free boundary defines critical point; visibility margin subtracts braking distance and yaw-recovery travel. Gatekeeper switches information-seeking yaw to velocity-aligned backup; positional HOCBF/MPC handles detected obstacles and peers. Theorem 3 assumes nonempty positional admissible set at all times. It does not establish unconstrained recursive feasibility of all stacked constraints or finite completion of every exploration task.

**Evidence (pp. 11–15):** 100 trials per allocator/team-size setting, R=1/2/3, two allocators, matched initial environments. Reported 100% completion and no collision; unknown-region overlap counts can remain nonzero and are distinct from physical collision. Isaac Sim and two-Crazyflie executions. Hardware sensing is a FoV event **simulated geometrically from Vicon**, not onboard camera perception (p. 14).

**Verified applicability discrepancy:** p. 15 prints v_max=1.35 m/s, a_max=1.5 m/s², l_range=0.25 m; p. 14 robot radius 0.075 m, obstacle radius 0.3 m. The stated worst-case braking distance is 0.6075 m, already exceeding l_range before radius/margin; these printed hardware parameters do not satisfy Assumption 3, (50), l_range >= r_safe + d_br (p. 10). Record as a theorem-to-experiment coverage question requiring clarification, not proof the observed flights are unsafe or the method impossible. Actual lower speeds/alternate interpretations may matter but are not supplied by that printed inequality.

**DBACT consequence:** unknown-space safety + finite sensing + backup + efficient progress already has an explicit recent framework. Distinctiveness must involve task-specific contact readiness/force geometry, a different certified sensing interface, or a concrete tighter result—not generic visibility-aware safe deployment.
